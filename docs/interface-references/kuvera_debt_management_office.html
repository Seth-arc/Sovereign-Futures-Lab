Republic of Kuvera

Common Framework treatment requestDebt assessed unsustainableIMF Board in 11 weeks

Kuvera country profile

Kuvera is a lower-middle-income economy in southeastern Africa, built on copper and cobalt. A price collapse, pandemic-era spending and reserve depletion pushed debt past the point the IMF now assesses as sustainable. Figures marked as case values are set for this exercise; where a quantity is not established, the profile says so rather than filling the gap.

Transcript

Fellow citizens. This morning my government formally asked our creditors to restructure the debts of this country. I want to tell you plainly why, because you will hear other versions of it.

Two of every five shillings this government collects comes from copper and cobalt. When the price fell, it did not fall on a balance sheet. It fell on clinics, on teachers’ salaries, on the road to the northern districts. More than a third of what we collect now leaves the country again as debt service. That is not a budget. That is a queue.

I have given our negotiators two instructions. The first is that we will not balance this on the backs of the people who did not borrow the money. The second is that we will not buy relief with concealment. If there are arrangements in our contracts that our other creditors do not know about, we will not pretend otherwise — a settlement built on something hidden is a settlement that fails twice.

This will take longer than any of us would like, and it will not be finished by the time the Fund meets. I ask for your patience, and I ask the people carrying this file for their honesty.

Common Framework: institutional context and simulation state

Where Kuvera sits in the Common Framework, and what still has to happen after it. The sequence below is the one this case runs on; real cases vary in order and timing, and the MoU template it references is illustrative rather than binding. The section to dwell on is the commitment ladder — the distance between a creditor saying yes and money actually changing hands.

What it isCoordinated official bilateral debt treatment

Official IMF background describes the G20 Common Framework as a mechanism for coordinated debt treatment requested by an eligible debtor country, bringing Paris Club and other G20 official bilateral creditors into a common process and requiring comparable treatment from other creditors.

What drives treatmentDSA + IMF-supported program parameters

The creditor committee negotiates treatment informed by the joint World Bank–IMF debt sustainability analysis and consistent with the parameters of the IMF-supported program.

Why financing assurances matterBoard consideration can precede final implementation

The RTLF distinguishes financing assurances from later agreement and implementation. An assurance is not the same as a final MoU or cash-effective relief.

Process boundaries used by the demo
  • Do not treat the illustrative commitment sequence as universal across every Common Framework case.
  • Do not equate financing assurances with final creditor agreements.
  • Do not equate an MoU with bilateral implementation.
  • Do not equate implementation progress with cash-effective relief.
  • Do not assign delay to one actor when approvals, data reconciliation, and technical work are distributed across the process.

What this demo can and cannot establish
  • It can show whether a participant distinguishes gross from usable liquidity in this scenario.
  • It can show whether they investigate account dependencies and explain a disclosure choice.
  • It cannot establish creditor motive, legality, or actual institutional conduct from the contract features.
  • It cannot infer competence or learning from one favorable outcome, one click, or one revision.
  • It cannot generalize Kuvera simulation behavior to real sovereign restructurings without independent external-validity evidence.
